How to use these pages

Work in this order, because each layer rests on the one before it. Accounting first — if you cannot link the three statements, nothing downstream will hold up. Then valuation, which is mostly a question of knowing which tool fits which situation. Then the DCF specifically, since it is the one walk-through you are almost certain to be asked for. LBO mechanics matter for private equity and for any banking role that touches leveraged finance. Markets questions are their own discipline: less about mechanics, more about whether you actually follow what is happening.

Do not try to learn all five in a week. A candidate who can answer accounting and valuation cleanly reads better than one with shaky coverage of everything.

Common follow-ups

What technical questions come up in a finance interview?
For investment banking: the three financial statements and how they link, how to walk through a DCF, the main valuation methodologies and when each applies, enterprise versus equity value, and basic LBO mechanics. For markets roles the emphasis shifts to where rates and major indices are, what moved them recently, and a stock or trade idea you can defend.
How do I prepare for a finance interview technically?
Drilling beats reading. Most candidates can recognise the right answer in an article and still freeze when asked out loud, because recognition and recall are different skills. Work through questions until you can answer without prompts, then practise saying the answer aloud in under two minutes.
How long should a technical answer be?
Ninety seconds to two minutes for a structured question like a DCF walk-through. Give the skeleton first, then offer detail: “project unlevered free cash flow, discount it at WACC, add a terminal value, bridge to equity value — want me to go deeper on any step?” Interviewers are testing structure as much as knowledge.
