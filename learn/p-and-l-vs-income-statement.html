P&L vs income statement: are they the same thing?

They are. Two names for one report — and the reason both exist is geography, not accounting.

5 min read

By Thomas Amara · 5 October 2026

Short answer

Yes — a P&L and an income statement are the same report. Two names, one statement: the one showing revenue, costs and profit over a period. “Profit and loss account” is the traditional British term; “income statement” is the formal term under IFRS and US GAAP.

If you have ever been handed a “P&L” by one person and an “income statement” by another and wondered which you were looking at, the answer is: the same thing. Nothing about the content differs. The split is linguistic, not accounting.

Why two names exist

UK company law used profit and loss account for decades, and it embedded itself in how British businesses talk. Accounting software, bookkeepers and managers still say P&L, and probably always will.

International and American standards settled elsewhere. US GAAP uses income statement; IFRS formally calls it the statement of profit or loss. Those are the terms in published accounts and in anything a regulator reads.

So the pattern in practice: the P&L is what people say, the income statement is what gets filed. You will also meet statement of operations and statement of earnings, both used by some US filers. All five names describe one report.

What is actually on it

Whatever it is called, the statement runs top to bottom from what came in to what was left. A simplified year might look like this.

American usage differs here too, harmlessly: revenue is often sales or net sales, cost of sales is cost of goods sold (COGS), and net profit is net income. Again, same lines.

What a P&L does not tell you

It does not show cash. A P&L is prepared on an accruals basis: revenue is recognised when earned, costs when incurred, regardless of when money moves. A company can report a healthy profit while its bank balance falls — customers who have not paid yet, inventory bought upfront, a loan repayment due.

That gap is precisely why there are three statements rather than one. The cash flow statement reconciles profit back to cash, and the balance sheet shows what the business owns and owes at a point in time. How the three link together is one of the most common questions in a finance interview.

Key takeaways

  • P&L and income statement are the same report under different names.
  • “P&L” is British and informal; “income statement” is the formal standard term.
  • It covers a period; a balance sheet covers a moment.
  • It shows profit, not cash — that is the cash flow statement’s job.

Common questions

Is a P&L the same as an income statement?
Yes. They are two names for the same financial statement: the one that shows revenue, costs and the resulting profit over a period of time. "Profit and loss account" is the traditional British term and the one still used in everyday business speech; "income statement" is the formal term under IFRS and US GAAP, and the one you will see in published accounts.
Why does the same statement have two names?
History and geography. UK company law used "profit and loss account" for decades, so it stuck in British usage. International and US accounting standards settled on "income statement" — IFRS formally calls it the statement of profit or loss. Software, bookkeepers and managers kept saying P&L; accountants and filings say income statement.
What is the difference between a P&L and a balance sheet?
A P&L covers a period — a month, quarter or year — and shows what you earned and spent during it. A balance sheet is a snapshot at a single date, showing what the business owns and owes at that moment. One is a video, the other a photograph.
Does a P&L show cash?
No, and this is the most common misunderstanding. A P&L is prepared on an accruals basis: revenue is recognised when earned, not when the money arrives, and costs when incurred, not when paid. That is why a third statement, the cash flow statement, exists to reconcile profit back to the cash that actually moved.
Is an income statement the same as a statement of operations?
Yes, that is a third name for it, used by some US filers. You may also see "statement of earnings", "statement of profit or loss" under IFRS, or simply "the P&L". All describe the same report.
What is the bottom line on a P&L?
Net profit, also called net income or profit after tax — what remains once every cost, including interest and tax, has been taken off revenue. The phrase "the bottom line" is literal: it is the last line of the statement.
