About

Thomas Amara

Founder of Basis, and the person who writes every lesson on it.

I fell in love with finance at fifteen and have been slightly obsessed ever since. Not with the idea of it — with the actual mechanics: why a bond falls when rates rise, why a bank can be profitable on Friday and gone by Monday, why the same company is worth two different numbers to two reasonable people.

Most of what I learned, I learned by doing rather than reading. Internships. Research projects. Investing my own money and being wrong often enough to take risk seriously. Running things inside student societies, where the gap between understanding a concept and being able to use it becomes obvious very quickly.

Why Basis exists

That gap is the whole reason this app exists.

Textbooks are good at explaining what a yield curve is. None of them make you decide what to do when it inverts. You can finish a finance degree able to define a discounted cash flow and still freeze when an interviewer says “walk me through one”, because recognising an answer and producing one are different skills and only one of them gets tested in an exam.

So Basis teaches the concept first — properly, with the material covering everything the quiz will ask — and then makes you use it. The Floor takes that further: five decisions a day drawn from real historical episodes, the same five for everyone, on a virtual book. SVB in March 2023. The gilt crisis. You size your conviction, you find out what actually happened, and you learn the thing no textbook teaches, which is what it feels like to be uncertain and have to act anyway.

What I do

I’m studying Business Management at the University of Southampton, graduating in 2027. I interned at the Financial Conduct Authority as a Summer Associate in Supervision, Policy and Competition, working on mainstream consumer credit lenders.

My research is in financial markets, macroeconomics and quantitative analysis — most recently a US bond market stress index built with hidden Markov models and UMAP. I invest on a philosophy of disciplined analysis, risk management, and patient conviction over short-term noise, which is roughly the attitude I have tried to build into The Floor.

More of that is on thomas-amara.com and LinkedIn.

Who writes the lessons

I do. All 98 of them, and all 449 questions. Nothing on Basis is generated and dropped in unread — every lesson leads with teaching that has to cover everything its quiz tests, and every scenario in The Floor is a real episode with a line explaining what actually happened, because a market simulation that invents its history teaches you nothing.

If something is wrong, I want to know. Tell me and I will fix it.
